Every pull request rebuilds the whole tree. Most of that output is byte-identical to a build that finished an hour ago on another machine.

CI spends most of its time rebuilding things that have not changed

A full build of the monorepo takes 14 minutes. A one-line change to a leaf package takes the same 14 minutes, because each CI runner starts from an empty working directory and no runner can see what any other runner has already produced.

Measured across one week of merges, 83% of compiled artifacts were byte-identical to an artifact produced by an earlier build. The work is not merely similar. It is the same bytes, computed again.

What it costs

Reviewer latency, not compute

Compute is cheap and getting cheaper. A reviewer who has context-switched away is expensive and does not come back for twenty minutes. The 14 minutes is the real number.

Why it persists

Local caches work, and do not travel

Every build tool already caches locally. That is why the problem is invisible to the person who introduced it: on their machine the second build takes nine seconds.
